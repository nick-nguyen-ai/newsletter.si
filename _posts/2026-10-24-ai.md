    headline: 'Agent Mosaic: agents combine confidential documents into conclusions they shouldn''t reveal'
    takeaway: Even with classification headers and rules, unsafe disclosures persisted in about a third of simulated answers.
    tags:
    - Data leakage
    - Agents
    - Paper
    sources:
    - Paper
    verdict: Worth reading
    what_changed: The paper tests the 'mosaic effect', where an agent reads several documents it is allowed to access and discloses a sensitive conclusion built from them. Across 30 synthetic cybersecurity, medical and military examples (10,800 simulated answers and 1,440 from live agents in two open-source harnesses), the best model tested, GPT-5.6-Sol, disclosed forbidden composites in 7.08% of answers to benign queries, with worse rates in live harnesses. Visible classification headers and explicit aggregation rules cut disclosures, but they persisted in 32.37% of simulated answers under that setup.
    why_it_matters: Document-level access control is how most bank RAG (retrieval-augmented generation) and agent deployments are approved. This shows that passing per-document checks doesn't stop an agent combining documents into something restricted, which is a test case worth adding to your validation suite.
    caveats: Synthetic examples only, and no banking data; the numbers are the authors' own.
