**Big picture**
- Oil jumped and AI stocks fell again, so Wall Street had its worst week since June. The Nasdaq lost 2.2% on Friday and the S&P 500 lost 1.1% ([Reuters via Financial Newsletter](https://financialnewsletter.com/wall-street-ends-down-47/)).
- The ASX went the other way on Friday, up 1% to 8,806.7 with tech and miners leading. That made it three winning weeks in a row ([CommBank/AAP](https://www.commbank.com.au/articles/newsroom/2026/10/asx-third-straight-weekly-gain-tech-miners-rebound.html)). It closed before Wall Street's sell-off, so Monday could start on the back foot.

**Macro and geopolitics**
- **US-Iran war:** Trump says he won't use nuclear weapons or send ground troops into Iran. CNN reports new US strike plans against Iran's energy and nuclear sites, which could be used before or after the 3 November midterms. On 8 October Trump said he wouldn't restart strikes before the midterms ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/10/16/us-iran-war-trump-hormuz.html)).
- **Hormuz:** Attacks on ships hit a record 12 in the week to 11 October, and oil flows through the strait are falling again ([BBC Verify](https://www.bbc.com/news/articles/cmde8dpg8g0no)). On Friday a tanker with Indian crew was hit by a projectile in the strait ([The National](https://www.thenationalnews.com/news/mena/2026/10/16/india-hormuz-strait-tanker-attack/)).

- **Oil:** Brent futures rose 3.7% on Friday to US$109.86, the highest since May. Unconfirmed reports of attacks on Saudi energy infrastructure drove the jump ([Reuters via Investing.com](https://www.investing.com/news/commodities-news/oil-edges-down-as-investors-weigh-possible-us-sanctions-on-shadow-fleet-4952926)). Good for ASX energy stocks, bad for inflation and travel stocks.
- **Trump and Russian diesel:** Trump announced a deal to lift sanctions on Russian diesel exports to bring fuel prices down before the midterms. Lawmakers question whether it's legal, and traders say the volumes are too small to lower pump prices much ([WSJ](https://www.wsj.com/livecoverage/stock-market-today-dow-sp-500-nasdaq-10-16-2026/card/u-s-oil-prices-rise-back-above-100-a-barrel-AJVtCoJZB6nzadgOjtIJ)).
- **Ukraine:** Russia hit nine regions, cutting power to hundreds of thousands of homes ([AP](https://apnews.com/article/russia-ukraine-war-trump-zelenskyy-power-grid-025bdb5cebc0be727a6197ce37f2c365)). With Kyiv at odds with Washington, Zelenskyy asked EU leaders for €70bn more next year ([Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/10/16/eu-rallies-in-support-of-ukraine-amid-trump-standoff)). Ukraine's military Starshield satellite internet also had a speed cap imposed without warning, and Kyiv says it has no explanation ([AP](https://apnews.com/article/starlink-starshield-ukraine-russia-war-shahed-spacex-pentagon-385eb944f6c34990bdf21f624416b820)).

- **Bonds and the Fed:** US inflation ran hot this week, with consumer prices up 3.7% on a year ago and producer prices up 6.3%. Even so, the 10-year yield barely moved from about 5.09%, and traders give more than an 80% chance the Fed holds rates this month ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/10/16/us-treasury-yields-investors-hot-inflation-data-.html)). Fed chair Warsh wouldn't say which way he leans: "I don't prejudge meetings."
- **RBA:** The cash rate is 4.60% after four hikes this year. The next decision is on 3 November, after the quarterly inflation figures come out on 28 October ([Aussie](https://www.aussie.com.au/insights/news/expert-predictions-rba-rates/)).

**Where analysts differ**
- **The Fed in October:** Markets see only about a 15% chance of a hike. CNBC's Matt Peterson warns they may be "dangerously complacent", because Warsh refuses to signal his moves in advance ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/10/16/kevin-warsh-fed-october-rate-hike-interest-rates-markets.html)). Westpac thinks the data is strong enough to justify an October hike, though Fed officials feel they have time ([Westpac](https://www.westpaciq.com.au/economics/2026/10/cliff-notes-16-oct-2026)). Barclays expects hikes in December and January instead ([Reuters](https://financialnewsletter.com/wall-street-ends-down-47/)).
- **RBA in November:** ANZ and Westpac expect a hike to 4.85%. CBA and NAB expect no change, though neither rules out a hike ([Aussie](https://www.aussie.com.au/insights/news/expert-predictions-rba-rates/)). Westpac still expects a hike even though unemployment is now above the RBA's comfort level ([Westpac](https://www.westpaciq.com.au/economics/2026/10/cliff-notes-16-oct-2026)).
- **AI stocks:** The bears worry that AI data-centre companies with big debts may not make profits in time to repay them. CoreWeave's shares are down about 25% in five days. The bulls point to analysts expecting US earnings to grow more than 30% this quarter, mostly thanks to AI giants ([Reuters](https://financialnewsletter.com/wall-street-ends-down-47/)).
- **Oil:** Energy Aspects says the ongoing risk of escalation will keep prices high. Kpler expects a slower, bumpier recovery in Hormuz flows without a political deal ([Reuters via Investing.com](https://www.investing.com/news/commodities-news/oil-edges-down-as-investors-weigh-possible-us-sanctions-on-shadow-fleet-4952926)).
