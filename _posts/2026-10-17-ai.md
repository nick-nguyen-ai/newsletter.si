## 1. Claude Code and Codex

**Claude Code Review is GA, and Anthropic explains how its own team reviews agent-written code**
Code Review left research preview for Team and Enterprise plans at 9:51am AEDT Friday, and most reviews now take 6 to 10 minutes. Several agents look for bugs in parallel, a verification step drops false positives, and the check run never approves, so people still decide the merge. The companion post is the real value: Claude writes about 90% of the team's code, and from June to September merged PRs more than doubled while the share reverted fell by more than 40%. They built a daily eval from past incidents, measure noise by how authors respond, cap review at three rounds, and never count an agent's approval as a human one.
For you: It's a near ready-made design for validating an LLM reviewer, with an incident-based regression set, a behaviour-based false-positive measure and a convergence rule. Reviews run on Anthropic's infrastructure, so check data rules first.

## 3. Evaluating and validating AI

**The FSB's final AI report: what the full text says about agents**
Unlike the draft covered on 15 Oct, the final text adds agent guidance the FSB says it didn't fully consult on, which may still change. Under Sound Practices 9 and 10, evaluating an agent means checking whether its actions were appropriate, not just whether it reached its goal. Monitoring should cover intermediate steps and manipulation attempts, and be "a key basis" for granting more autonomy. Testing should cover the whole workflow, with red teaming for higher-autonomy agents. Firms should set thresholds in advance, cross-check agents' own summaries, test kill switches, evaluate AI monitors, and give each agent a unique ID.
For you: Use Practices 9 and 10 as a gap check. Kill-switch tests, pre-set thresholds and evidence your LLM-judge monitors work are the likeliest gaps.
[FSB final report (PDF)](https://www.fsb.org/uploads/P151026-1.pdf) · [Final report page](https://www.fsb.org/2026/10/sound-practices-for-responsible-adoption-of-artificial-intelligence-ai-final-report/) · [Overview of consultation responses](https://www.fsb.org/2026/10/sound-practices-for-responsible-adoption-of-artificial-intelligence-ai-overview-of-consultation-responses/)

## 5. Big AI companies

**Anthropic: Claude worked out where the NIST elliptic-curve seeds came from**
In under half an hour, an internal research model showed that the "random" seeds behind NIST's P-192 to P-521 curves were ordinary `lrand48()` output seeded with Unix timestamps from May 1997 to April 1998. P-256 comes from `srand48(871696354)`, on 16 Aug 1997. It spotted a top-bit-zero pattern, brute-forced 17 hidden bits and used LLL lattice reduction to recover the generator state. Anthropic stresses this breaks nothing. Filippo Valsorda says only about 30 bits of freedom were left, ruling out a planted weak curve, and he now recommends the NIST curves. Steve Weis says Claude also traced 1977 DES test vectors to `rand()` on a PDP-11.
For you: This is agentic research you can trust, since every claim ends in something anyone can rerun and check with no judge in the loop.
[Anthropic: The origins of the NIST curve seeds](https://www.anthropic.com/research/ecdsa-curve-origins) · [Filippo Valsorda: Seeds solved](https://words.filippo.io/seeds-solved/)

## 8. Trending AI papers

**FDE-Gym: can agents deliver for a real customer, not just close a ticket?**
FDE-Gym copies a forward-deployed engineer's job rather than a well-specified issue, with 95 delivery tasks from 20 customer scenarios across 18 sectors. Agents talk to scripted stakeholders, change production-like systems, and are scored on the customer's own success metric, with penalties for messages, trial runs and waiting. The best of 13 model configurations averages 0.56 and fully resolves only 14 tasks. Unstated constraints are the main bottleneck, but even on 23 tasks where it got every key fact, the best configuration reached 0.8 or above on only 10. It's #4 on HF Daily Papers.
For you: Use it as a template for ambiguous requests. Score the business outcome, cost each clarifying question, and check whether the agent asked about unstated constraints.
[arXiv 2610.22350](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.22350) · [HF Papers](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.22350)

**OpenGCUA: an open stack for computer-use agents that write code**
Tongyi-MAI's open computer-use agent lets the model choose between clicking through the GUI and running code, and hands some decisions about what to observe and track from fixed harness rules to the model. On OSWorld-v2 it improved all eight models tested, raising mean partial score by 19.6 points and cutting steps per task by 53.0% against batched GUI-only execution. On real macOS tasks, some Qwen3.8-Max runs used 19.4 times as many reasoning tokens as GPT-6 Astra. It's #3 on HF Daily Papers.
For you: Validation results belong to the model plus harness pair, not the model alone. Track token cost per task too, since it varies widely between models.
[arXiv 2610.22200](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.22200) · [HF Papers](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.22200)

**Self-Evolving Agent Harness: rules for letting agents rewrite their own scaffolding**
This 66-page framework and survey formalises "harness self-evolution", where an agent's prompts, memory, workflows, tools and code are revised from feedback while the weights stay fixed, and accepted changes persist. Its admission rule only accepts a revision that holds up on fresh, independent validation data, within bounds on regression. The authors argue deployment needs continual validation as tasks and evaluators drift, plus independent safeguards on privileges and consequential actions. It's #6 on HF Daily Papers.
For you: Self-updating skills, memory files and AGENTS.md rules are common in coding agents. Gate each persistent change on a held-out set and keep a regression budget.
[arXiv 2610.18813](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.18813) · [HF Papers](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.18813)

**MAI-Gym: mocked apps that train GUI agents for the real thing**
MAI-Gym uses one rollout pipeline for evaluation, data collection and online RL across mobile, desktop and web. It synthesises resettable mocked apps with execution-validated task verifiers. Its 9B model scores 78.4% on MobileWorld and 76.2% on OSWorld-Verified, the best open GUI agent under 10B parameters. RL in a mocked train-ticketing app carried over to the real one, lifting success on a physical phone from 28.8% to 48.1%. It's #11 on HF Daily Papers.
For you: Resettable mocks of internal systems with execution-validated checkers can stand in for production in agent tests, and one shared pipeline removes a source of drift.
[arXiv 2610.20939](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.20939) · [HF Papers](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.20939)

**Data World: synthetic data-analysis tasks with checkable answers**
Training and testing data agents, which write code over messy files to find insights, hits privacy limits and hard-to-verify answers. Data World defines the "world" behind the files first and then generates them, so the ground truth is known, and audits check the answer is recoverable. Its 100-task benchmark is hard for ten frontier models. RL on 2,135 synthesised tasks improved Qwen3.6-35B-A3B by 7.06 points on DataWorld-Bench, 14.28 on DAComp and 20.90 on DABstep-Hard.
For you: Generating data from a known mechanism, then auditing that answers are recoverable, is a clean way to build synthetic test sets where customer data is off-limits.
[arXiv 2610.21656](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.21656) · [HF Papers](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.21656)

**JevAgentHarness: handing tool choices to a small, cheap decision model**
This paper asks whether a small decision model can make an agent's multiple-choice decisions, such as which tool to call next. The model most accurate on isolated questions solved only 69.5% of agent tasks, and delegating without a fitted confidence threshold cut success by 19.0 points. The fix is a per-choice threshold fitted on the agent's own trajectories, with the big LLM deciding below it. Open 4B models trained on post-mistake states made agents 81.1–89.0% cheaper and up to 2.5 times faster on similar tasks, losing under 3 points of success.
For you: Accuracy on isolated tool-selection questions didn't predict end-to-end success, so test tool routing inside full trajectories, including the recovery states after errors.
[arXiv 2610.21412](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.21412) · [HF Papers](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.21412)

**SWEeper-Bench: can coding agents find bugs nobody has reported yet?**
Princeton built 200 tasks from real bug fixes in 200 widely used open-source web apps. The agent gets only the codebase and a vague request such as "Find and fix issues in Focalboard's board sharing and membership", so it must design interactive tests to surface the bug, and an agentic verifier judges repairs by what a user would see. The best of 15 frontier agents passes only 59.0%, often reaching the right feature but never triggering the failure, or not recognising it.
For you: When Claude Code or Codex "checks" an app, it may never exercise the failing path. Ask for interaction-level tests and judge by observable behaviour.
[arXiv 2610.14096](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.14096) · [HF Papers](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.14096)
